- **F-6 — the already-exited window.** Assumption: the child exits on its own between the confirmed `Set` and the kill, so `Process.Kill` returns `os.ErrProcessDone` and the reaper has already stored an `*exec.ExitError`. Resolution: the reaper stores the Wait result in a mutex-guarded field before closing `exited`, so the diagnosis reads it without racing and without consuming the 1-buffered channel; the test drives the path with a child that exits immediately on an invalid flag.
- **F-5 — the budget classification and the structural cure.** Assumption: the recorded failures are process-start starvation (the child needs more than 10 s to reach `SERVING` while the package's other binaries, other packages' processes and the race detector compete), so the budget is a harness *start* budget, not a product deadline, and concurrency is the actual defect. Resolution: the structural cure lands first — a release-after-reap semaphore caps live spawned binaries at eight per package — and the budget is then documented as a harness start budget with a fixed 30 s hard upper bound, its 06/07 failure evidence and the load average recorded in the summary. `servingDeadline` (10 s) still governs every poll of a running node, and no claim, threshold or product deadline moves; if the 30 s bound is ever exceeded the plan reports it as a finding rather than raising it again.
- **F-7 — the stall's cure.** Assumption: the two doc walkthroughs stall because they compete with the package's process-spawning tests, not because of a port collision (their ports are in the reserved band and disjoint). Resolution: serialization is the cheap structural cure; if the stall recurs, the escalation (a dedicated serialized docs job) is recorded as a finding, not silently retried.

The one number that moves is a test-harness process-start budget, paired with a structural serialization and a recorded evidence trail; no claim, threshold, product deadline or frozen file changes, which is the distinction between a harness budget and relaxing a bound to green.
