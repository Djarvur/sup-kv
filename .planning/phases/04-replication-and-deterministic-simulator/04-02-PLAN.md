<objective>
The reliability layer of the replication core: per-peer (key, version) tracking with
exponential-backoff retransmission, suspension while no peer is known, drain on Ack or
supersession, and the full N-peer table lifecycle including peer_timeout eviction
(REPL-02, REPL-06).

Purpose: a write is never silently forgotten — it is retransmitted on a documented ladder
until every known peer has Acked it or a strictly greater version supersedes it, and the
peer table keeps that tracking correct as peers appear and disappear. This plan is
core-only: the adapter's event loop and timer re-arm from plan 04-01 already drive
everything this plan adds inside Advance.
Output: expanded internal/app/repl/core.go (pending sets, backoff ladder, suspension,
flush-on-discovery, peer eviction, PendingSnapshot/UnackedStats accessors) plus
retransmit_test.go and peertable_test.go.
</objective>

    1. Ladder in Advance: an entry becomes due when now is at or after nextRetry AND the peer table is non-empty. Firing = one Broadcast DataReplication for the entry's record (every peer shares each send; peers that already Acked re-merge idempotently and re-Ack — the plan-01 always-Ack-on-duplicate truth). After firing set backoff = min(2*backoff, cfg.AckTimeoutMax) and nextRetry = now + backoff; the ladder delays from the initial cfg.AckTimeout are therefore 200ms, 400ms, 800ms, 1600ms, 3200ms, then 5000ms repeated forever — document the full ladder and the saturation rule in the doc comment of the scan (clock.go counter-overflow precedent), citing REPL-02 and the A5 reading: retransmit_interval is the scan granularity — while any entry is pending, the deadline returned by Advance is never more than cfg.RetransmitInterval past now, so a due retry is honored at scan granularity; with no pending entries the retransmit scanner contributes no deadline.
    2. Suspension: when the peer table is empty the scanner fires nothing and does NOT advance nextRetry or backoff — entries are retained unchanged (the timers are effectively paused; REPL-02 "suspending (not dropping)").
    3. Flush-on-discovery in the Hello handler: when a Hello arrives and the peer table was empty just before it, broadcast every pending entry in that same HandleDatagram call (sorted by key), add the new peer to every wait-set, and reset every ladder (backoff = cfg.AckTimeout, nextRetry = now + cfg.AckTimeout). A Hello that does not end an empty table adds the peer to wait-sets of pending entries only via this same rule on discovery — peers already known do not modify wait-sets. This is the A6 reading: a brand-new peer receives the pending set promptly, not at the next backoff expiry.
    4. Emission order: every place this task emits multiple datagrams in one handler call (flush, a tick with several due entries) iterates keys sorted ascending — the deterministic-emit discipline (probe #9, RESEARCH Pitfall 1); document the requirement at the scan's doc comment.
    5. Advance's returned next deadline becomes the minimum over: the hello timer, the retransmit scan ceiling (now+RetransmitInterval when entries are pending, clamped by the earliest nextRetry), the peer-timeout scan (Task 3), and any other armed timer — never a zero/now value: if work is due immediately the handlers fire inside this same Advance call before returning a strictly-future deadline.
